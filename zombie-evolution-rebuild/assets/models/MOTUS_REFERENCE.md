# Motus pistol animation reference

The user supplied `FBX_Pistol_Starter_27A (1).zip` via Google Drive (SHA-256 `9d5a6ff26a0e70fa36625b9ff3fab2cbffad7d8a4200a0ce12a225d87bcc5559`) and confirmed permission to use the files and place converted versions in this repository. The archive identifies MocapOnline/MotusMan v55; no independent license text was included. Preserve the user's permission record and do not infer broader licensing terms.

`motus-pistol-reference.glb` is a test-only conversion of the source mannequin and three FBX animations: standing pistol aim idle, walking while aiming, and standing single shot. Conversion uses assimpjs `glb2`, followed by `ci/assemble_motus_reference.py` to map animation targets by unique node name and embed the supplied `MCG_diff.jpg` texture. This conversion can alter FBX fidelity and needs visual and runtime review. It is not an authored rifle animation and no weapon or hand IK is supplied by this conversion. It has not been retargeted or integrated into hero/squad gameplay. The existing hero and its reported rig and grip problems are unchanged. The project's all-resources export may include this staged reference, increasing APK size; remove from shipping exports or choose a selective export once the asset is no longer needed for testing.

The original archive notes that MotusMan v55 and v4 skeletons differ; do not assume animations from other packs match. Test skeletal mapping and proportions explicitly before retargeting. Presence of a clip or imported bones is not visual proof of natural motion, correct two-handed rifle placement, transition blending, or phone performance.

## Validation and visual review

[Run 43779025385](https://github.com/kamandbeauty/Ideal/actions/runs/43779025385), commit `8c5190c`, passed headless, rendered and Android jobs. The added tests establish that a skeleton and named clips import and change bone transforms, not that the conversion is visually correct. The test-only walk pose was rendered in [run 43779611307](https://github.com/kamandbeauty/Ideal/actions/runs/43779611307) and its reduced still was inspected: the mannequin appears strongly distorted and incorrectly oriented, with elongated limbs. The cause is not yet established (conversion, skeletal transforms, scene setup, or a combination); this output must not be used in gameplay. Inspect the original FBX in a compatible editor, fix conversion and verify multiple moving poses before attempting retargeting. A still cannot establish animation quality. No physical-device test was performed.

## Alternative conversion experiment

To isolate Assimp's FBX helper-node handling, `motus-fbx2gltf-reference.glb` was independently converted from the same three FBX clips with the npm `fbx2gltf` 0.9.7-p1 Linux binary (FBX2glTF 0.9.7, Autodesk FBX SDK) and `ci/assemble_motus_fbx2gltf.py`. The input files' missing external texture references were replaced with the supplied JPEG. The resulting GLB is a separate candidate, not a replacement for the prior conversion and not used in gameplay. FBX2glTF emits a 75-node hierarchy rather than Assimp's 270-node hierarchy for the idle source and writes metre-scale geometry. Neither this structural difference nor passing tests establishes visual or animation quality. Visual review is required before deciding whether it is usable for retargeting.

[Run 43815376352](https://github.com/kamandbeauty/Ideal/actions/runs/43815376352), commit `1de18b3`, passed headless, rendered and Android jobs. Test-only side-angle stills of the alternative conversion were inspected for walking aim and firing: the mannequin stands upright with recognizable body proportions and arms raised, unlike the severely distorted Assimp conversion. This is evidence of improved static import, not proof of correct animation over time or proof that conversion is fully accurate. The mannequin is wearing a stylized bodysuit, its hands are empty, and neither a rifle nor a support-hand IK solution was tested. The alternative reference remains test-only; hero/squad gameplay and the reported rifle grip are unchanged. Motion, retargeting, weapons, visual quality and Android device performance remain unverified.

## Experimental weapon attachment and support-hand IK

`ci/motus_rifle_experiment.gd` is an isolated test scene: the Quaternius rifle follows the Motus right-hand bone attachment, with a separate left-arm SkeletonIK3D targeting a point on the rifle. This is not connected to the player, squad, firing, hit detection or gameplay. It uses a pistol animation and its rifle grip points are approximate. A side-angle test-only still from [run 43956949032](https://github.com/kamandbeauty/Ideal/actions/runs/43956949032), commit `610cc5d`, was inspected: the rifle is raised in front of the mannequin, but the barrel is cropped, no finger grip is confirmed, and placement is not production-ready. The measured right attachment was approximately 0.004 m from its configured grip, while the left hand attachment was approximately 0.187 m from the support target in the sampled frame. The solver running alone does not prove contact; the support-hand problem is **not fixed**. The measurement is a single sampled frame and needs further checks through entire walk and firing cycles, on reachable targets, and from several views.

The headless, rendered and Android CI jobs passed, checking initialization, the right-hand attachment, target tracking, the IK being active, and existing gameplay tests. These checks do not assert actual left-hand contact or motion quality. An actual Android device was not tested.

## Support target reachability follow-up

The initial support target was beyond the arm's reach. In the solver's `modification_processed` callback, sampled root-to-target distances were approximately 0.75–0.77 m, while the upper-arm/forearm chain measured about 0.573 m; this explains much of the earlier gap. The target was moved closer to the right-hand grip and toward the supporting side, near the receiver rather than at the previous forward point. With this revised test-only placement, post-modifier measurements in [run 44935720043](https://github.com/kamandbeauty/Ideal/actions/runs/44935720043), commit `4da3366`, showed approximately 1.0–1.6 cm separation for the visible sampled walk, idle and firing poses. CI headless, rendered and Android jobs passed. The capture is a small side-angle still and the rifle muzzle is cropped: it does not prove contact with the surface, finger placement, full-cycle behavior, recoil, a convincing rifle pose or a proper foregrip. The closer target reduces the numerical error but puts the support hand near the receiver, not necessarily at a realistic foregrip. The modified code is still test-only and does not change the gameplay hero. Device performance and motion remain unverified.
